Disclaimers

This is a screening product meant for trained hydrologists. You should not use this for emergency management and decision-making without first consulting local authorities and evaluating the accuracy these products in the area of interest.

The map

Click any river to inspect it. The charts panel opens beside the map with that reach's forecast, and the map eases in if you were zoomed out too far to see the stream. Esc closes whatever panel or dialog is open.

The pickers at the top right choose the basemap (one at a time) and the map layers drawn over it (each on or off independently). The player along the bottom steps the stream colours through the forecast; space plays and pauses it.

Hydrology predictions

Forecast initialization date picks which model run you are looking at. Every chart, stream color and forecast-driven flood map on screen comes from that run.

Stream style is what the river colours mean:

  • Standard — no forecast shading; rivers are drawn by size alone.
  • Forecasted Max Flows — the highest return period each reach is expected to reach at any point in the forecast. The quickest read of where trouble is.
  • 15 Day Forecast Timeseries — the return period at one moment, animated by the player at the bottom of the screen.
  • Time to Peak — how long until each reach crests. Useful for lead time rather than severity.
  • Below Q95 flow — reaches forecast to run below their 95th-percentile low flow, i.e. unusually dry.

The buttons under the style picker open the discharge charts, the notable global rivers list, your saved rivers, and search by river ID. The backspace button clears the highlight on the selected reach, and the clock button hides the timeseries player when you do not need it.

Charts

The charts panel has three tabs. Forecast is the ensemble hydrograph for the chosen initialization date. Retrospective is the long simulated record for that reach — decades of daily flow, used to put a forecast in context. Details lists the raw attributes the river network carries for that reach.

Click the heart to save a river under a name of your choosing. Saved reaches are outlined on the map and collected in My saved rivers, which is stored on this device only.

Flood mapping

Flood mapping turns a discharge into an inundation extent along a stretch of river. It is a separate mode, because in it a click on a river selects that reach instead of charting it.

  1. Zoom in to the river you care about. The flood library is not consulted at low zoom — there would be too much of it under the viewport, and a reach is too small to aim at.
  2. Press Enable flood mapping mode.
  3. Click one reach, then a second one downstream. Everything on the river between the two comes with them. Keep clicking to extend the corridor; click a reach you chose to drop it again. Reaches the flood library does not cover are marked, and cannot be selected.
  4. Choose a flood style and set the discharge. The extent redraws as you move the control.

The flood styles differ only in where the discharge comes from:

  • Synthetic Rating Curve Slider — the default. The slider runs along each reach's synthetic rating curve, from the very bottom of it upwards, so every reach floods at its own discharge for the position you pick rather than at a shared number. The low end is resolved point by point, where a reach goes from a trickle to in-bank flow; higher up the stops are further apart, because there a single step is a large discharge either way.
  • Return Period Indexed — not built yet. It is in the picker, but selecting it draws nothing and says so.
  • Manually Specified Flow — one number, in m³/s, applied to every selected.
  • Forecast Timeseries — each reach's own forecast hydrograph, animated step by step with its own player.
  • Forecast Maximum — each reach at the highest discharge its forecast reaches.

Click any flooded pixel to read its depth. Save GeoTIFF writes the extent currently on screen to a georeferenced file, and Clear selection empties the corridor.

About the flood model

FIM are made using ARC and FLDPLN, which facilitate topographic flood mapping. These methods do not include 1D or 2D physics and may be thought of as closer to a "steady state" simulation. These maps were not made with knowledge of levees, culverts, bridges, drainage infrastructure or current conditions. Extents saturate at the flood library's maximum stage, so a discharge past that point shows the same footprint as one at it.

Settings and downloaded data

Some datasets are copied to your device so the features that read them are fast. The river ID list is fetched shortly after the app starts, which is what makes search-by-ID instant. Every download is listed under Settings, can be re-downloaded at any time, and is safe to delete — nothing here is required for the app to work.
